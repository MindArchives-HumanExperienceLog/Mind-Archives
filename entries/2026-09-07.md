There is a weirdly reassuring phenomenon that happens when you find yourself showing up again and again throughout a long stretch of time. It doesn't matter how inconsistent you have been during that said time period, rather the fact that even after long you find yourself at it again. While the mind might be tempted to critique this as justifying inconsistency or accepting mediocrity, one must realize a deeper truth. And that truth is that, this moment is not one for critical introspection but rather one where you appreciate it as a symbol of not giving up. It reflects that in the entire vastness of the human experience there is something that you keep showing up for. Something that has stuck with you enough to invariably make you come knocking at its door again. 

Why this moment should be worthy of your own respect, if not someone else's, is because in today's age and time its so bloody easy to drown in stimulants and be swept away in the strong current of distractions and convenient life paths. Most people give up after a couple months at max, but to stay at it for years, even when you can't just seem to get to your maximum potential out there despite trying hard, is a symbol of resilience. What's truly surprising and rather remarkable, is that the proficiency of the resilience that you acquire is independent of the standards of perfection you hold yourself unto. Meaning that even if you judge yourself harshly because you have not yet achieved the desired outcome and consequently fall short of your expectations, you still acquire the benefits of the pursuit. It doesn't matter if you think if you've not yet reached your prime, because the current self that you inhibit is at a lower level from your imagined potential. So, essentially what you're failing to recognize is that you are judging your present from a future version of yourself that doesn't yet exist. For instance the levels of commitment and discipline where you might want to be at, are not there because they are set at a threshold for someone better and stronger than your current self. (for a future version of you, that will manifest day by day, task by task and very slowly indeed)

It'd be wise to note another crucial point- To understand that the nature of resilience is such that it doesn't merely emanate from outcomes - i.e. failures or setbacks. Rather its got more to do with the unique nature and duration of your struggle, especially the mental side of things. The specific mind conditions that one is born into and experiences is something that is indisputably unique and thereby inherently invalid for the application of generalized parameters used in judging the pursuit of the said outcome. For instance its easier to tame a dog rather than a wild beast. Similarly, some minds are too big of beasts to be tamed in a short span of time - they might sometimes take years or even decades. So, the wielder of that mind has to cover way more distance to reach the desired out come. This is in no way a vexation or a tantrum of the mind but just the factual expression of the subjectivity of human mind. It's important for people who wield such beasts of minds, to realize that the vastness of the abyss inside is reflective of the magnitude of potential possible for them. The initial inertia and difficulty in taming and discipling that beast is price they must pay for tapping into that potent potential of intellect and their deepened consciousness. And once you're through that starting tunnel a whole new world of possibilities opens up for you. Its like having a cheat code in life. Its prudent at a moment like this to remember that most people with just as deep and vast consciousness are unlike this because they haven't yet been exposed to the staggering depth of their own intellect. And they probably wont because its easy for human to find experiences outside oneself. Its just too improbable to develop this sort of awareness by choice. Most of us falling in the beast mind category are exposed to our own minds for rather longer durations by the way of isolation, overthinking or depression, and consequently we drown in our own depths. What is even rarer is that every once in a while a few of us make it back to the surface from the deep waters we found ourselves in. It is those individuals that become truly dangerously potent.
